## 2. Synthesis

### RQ4 — wait weights, headway dependence, reliability and RTI

**Initial wait weight.** By stream:
- S2 meta-analyses (UK): 1.47 (Wardman 2004) to 2.0 (Abrantes & Wardman 2011). Median of the two ≈1.7. The 1.76 and Wardman-2016 values in the brief were not re-found in any snippet and are not in the table.
- S1 guidance: ATAP 1.4 (platform wait; up to 1.75–2.0 congested); TCQSM 2.0; older WebTAG 2.5 (per TfL); Kol-TRAST (Sweden) 2.0 actual / 1.0 hidden; Netherlands 2022 national study ≈1.0 (not significantly different from IVT); TAG M3.2 Table 1 ranges NOT confirmed (see H01). Median ≈2.0, range 1.0–2.5.
- S3 primary: TfL London bus 2016 = 2.0 overall; 1.7 / 1.0 / 0.8 with mobile / internet / both RTI.
- S4 models (verified files): MTC family 2.0 (≤10 min) then 1.0; ARC 2.0 (work) / 1.33 (non-mandatory); MWCOG 1.49–1.50 (work, school, non-mandatory), 2.77 at-work; SANDAG ABM3 1.5 all purposes. Unverified: Railplan 2.0, Sound Transit 2.0. Median of verified work-purpose values = 1.5–2.0.
- Overall: the model's current W_WAIT = 2.0 sits at the upper half of the empirical evidence (1.0–2.0) and at the typical practice value (1.5–2.5). The MOT 2–3 range is above most empirical estimates but consistent with Abrantes & Wardman, TCQSM, Railplan, Sound Transit and the MTC family.

**Transfer wait vs initial wait.** The direction is not consistent across sources: ATAP (1.5 vs 1.4), MWCOG (1.53 vs 1.49; 2.0 vs 1.5 non-mandatory), SANDAG (2.0 vs 1.5) weight transfer wait higher; ARC weights it lower (1.5 vs 2.0); MTC equal (2.0 vs 2.0 for short initial wait, but transfer wait has no 10-min step-down). Across verified files the transfer-wait multiplier is 1.5–2.0 (median ≈2.0 for work). A single wait weight of 2.0 for both is within range; if differentiated, a modest premium of +0.0 to +0.5 on transfer wait is the typical pattern.

**Headway dependence / wait rule.** (a) Half-headway for random arrivals is the universal default (Emme α=0.5; Sound Transit 0.50–0.60; "traditional models"). (b) The random-arrival threshold in empirical studies is 5–11 min (Ingvardson et al. 2018 range; Fan & Machemehl 11 min; Luethi: some timetable use even at 5 min; London Underground 2–3 min; TCQSM 10 min; TAG M3.2 "10–15 min"). (c) Caps: TAG's "7.5 min beyond 15-min headway" was not confirmed. Emme guidance explicitly advises against clipping headway and recommends lower wait factors at infrequent nodes; ActivitySim MTC implements the equivalent by weighting initial wait beyond 10 min at 1.0 instead of 2.0 (ARC uses a 7.5-min threshold with no weight change). Implication for the Haifa model: the 10-min cap on ½-headway wait is defensible and sits inside the 5–15 min threshold band, but a weight step (2.0 → 1.0 beyond 10 min) is the better-documented mechanism than a hard time cap. At the LRT's 5-min headway the expected wait of 2.5 min may be slightly overstated (Luethi; Ingvardson 43% timely at 5 min).

**Headway multiplier.** Only Wardman 2004: 0.80 ± 0.08 × IVT per minute of headway (meta). PDFH frequency-penalty values were not retrieved.

**Reliability.** Treated outside the wait weight in all sources found: Bowman & Turnquist (wait more sensitive to reliability than frequency; timetable-aware wait ∝ schedule deviation); ATAP includes a separate reliability term in generalised time; TAG M3.2 formula includes crowding but no reliability term appeared in the snippet; Kol-TRAST "hidden wait" at 1.0. No numeric reliability weight (e.g., SD multiplier) was retrieved.

**Real-time information.** Reduces perceived wait (Watkins 2011: RTI users' perceived ≈ measured, non-users overestimate; Brakewood: −1.5 to −2 min reported wait, −16%), reduces actual wait (Watkins: ≈2 min less), and lowers the wait *weight* (TfL 2016: 2.0 → 1.7 mobile, 1.0 internet, 0.8 both). Perceived/actual ratios without RTI: 1.35–2.03 (Athens). For a new LRT with countdown displays and app coverage this supports using a wait weight at or below 2.0, not above.

### RQ5 — pure bus–bus transfer penalty (confirmation)

- Pure penalties net of walk and wait: London RP 5.03 min; Madrid SP 10.9 min; review range 4–20 min; ATAP guidance 5 (same facility) / 7 (different) / 5–10; ActivitySim MTC 10 min per transfer; SANDAG ABM3 ≈1.9–2.4 min per transfer (but with transfer walk ×2.5 and wait ×2.0); MWCOG 0 (penalty carried by wait weights only).
- Totals (including walk/wait, or mixed): Dublin 16–21 min; Currie review bus–bus 22 min (one snippet) / 30–50 min (other snippet) vs 8 min metro–metro; Sweden 2–17 min total; Scotland 4.5 min.
- Median of pure-penalty estimates ≈ 5–10 min; the model's 8 generalised minutes is inside the range and inside ATAP's 5–10. Bus–bus penalties are consistently higher than rail–rail (Currie; Iseki & Taylor), attributed to reliability, exposure and road crossing — relevant to Haifa's bus-feeder legs.

- Net: Israel provides a policy range (2–3 OVT/IVT) only; there is no Israeli empirical wait/transfer evidence in the public domain found here.
