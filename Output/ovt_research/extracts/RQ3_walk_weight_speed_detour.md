# RQ3 — Walk-time multiplier, walk speed, detour and slope adjustments for PT access/egress/transfer walking (Haifa LRT parameter review)

## 0. How this evidence was gathered and what "verified" means here

- 45 WebSearch queries were run (English, Hebrew, Swedish, Danish, German). WebFetch was blocked for every host tried (infocenters.co.il for PRAT 2012, eprints.whiterose.ac.uk for Wardman 2004, oecd.org for ITF 2014), so **no document was read in full**. Every number in the table was taken from a search-result summary or snippet; all rows are therefore marked **search-summary (unverified)**. Where a summary was internally inconsistent I say so in the "context" column rather than resolving it myself.
- Numbers I was asked to check but could NOT confirm from any snippet: a walk multiplier value in Abrantes & Wardman 2011 (only the wait ≈2× result surfaced); any explicit walk/wait weight in Israel's PRAT 2012 (only a "wait perceived 2–4× IVT" statement surfaced); any Danish TERESA weight; TCQSM's own 3 mph statement (only third-party restatements surfaced); the "walking tolerance of transitway users" (Minnesota LRT) distances; Emme/VISUM default weights; German Standardisierte Bewertung weights. These are listed as gaps, not estimates.

## 2. Synthesis

**Walk weight by stream (all values × IVT, from the table):**
- *Empirical meta-analyses and national SP/RP studies (S2/S3):* Wardman 2001/2004 1.66; Wardman et al. 2016 1.45 urban / 1.70 inter-urban; Wardman et al. 2026 worldwide 1.50–1.75; TRL593 range 1.4–2.0; Netherlands 2014 ≈1.5 and 2022 ≈1.5–2.0; Norway 2020 ≈1.3. Median of these central values ≈ **1.5–1.66**; overall range ≈ 1.3–2.0. The recurring message from the Wardman series is that the empirical multiplier sits below the "2×" convention; the 2026 worldwide meta-analysis explicitly recommends 1.75 (RP-weighted) or 1.50 (SP-weighted). RP-based values are systematically higher than SP-based ones.
- *Guidance / appraisal conventions (S1):* TAG M3.2 1.5–2.0; ATAP 1.5; TfNSW 1.5 (normal effort, uncongested); Sweden ASEK/Kol-TRAST 2; FTA 2–3 (OVT in mode choice); Israel PRAT 2012: wait 2–4 (walk not surfaced). Median ≈ **2.0** (range 1.5–3).
- *Calibrated / applied models (S4):* NYC MTA 1.5; Metrolinx 1.5–2.5; MWCOG 2.0; STIF 2.0; ANTONIN 2.0 (access/egress) and 2.5 (transfer walk); Hong Kong MTR 2.0. Median ≈ **2.0** (range 1.25–2.5).
- Practical implication for Haifa: a walk weight of ~1.5 is defensible on the empirical evidence (and matches TAG's lower bound and ATAP/TfNSW), 2.0 is the modelling convention; TfNSW's qualifier ("1.5 for normal effort, uncongested") and the 2.5 transfer-walk value in ANTONIN suggest using the upper part of the range for strenuous (steep) access walks and for transfer walking.

**Does the walk weight differ for rail/LRT vs bus?** Evidence is thin and partly contradictory. (i) Wardman 2004 Table 11, as summarised, gives mode-specific implied walk multipliers of 2.15 (bus) vs 1.65 (rail) at a 5-mile trip, but the same summary says "walk time value varies little by mode" — this needs checking in the paper. (ii) TRL593 says walk weights (1.4–2.0) show "no obvious dependence on trip type and main mode", whereas wait differs (bus 1.6, rail 1.2). (iii) TAG M3.2 does not give mode-specific walk weights but weights tram/LRT IVT at 0.7–1.1 vs bus 1–1.4 (rail = 1), so relative to an LRT in-vehicle minute the effective walk multiplier is higher than relative to a bus minute. (iv) The clearest mode difference is behavioural rather than in the weight: observed walk distances to rail are ~1.75–2.4× those to bus (Sydney mean 805 m vs 461 m; Montreal 85th pct 1,259 m vs 524 m), supporting longer walk catchments (800 m vs 400 m convention) for LRT even if the per-minute weight is held constant. No LRT-specific SP walk-weight estimate surfaced.

**Walk speed and detour:** Conventions cluster at 1.2 m/s (4.3 km/h, journey planners), 3 mph ≈ 4.8 km/h ≈ 80 m/min (US planning, 400 m in 5 min), 4 km/h plus 2-min constant and 1 km cap in the Israeli national model (per Amedi 2023), Tobler flat ≈5 km/h; design/crossing speeds are lower (MUTCD 3.5 ft/s = 1.07 m/s; Knoblauch 15th-percentile 1.25 m/s young, 0.97 m/s older). Detour/circuity factors used in catchment work are 1.2–1.5 (1.2 common; 1.15 used as a "near-shortest" threshold, >1.5 flagged as poor directness); Andersen & Landex's rule of thumb equates a network catchment to 0.7× the nominal straight-line radius, 0.6× in hilly terrain.

**Slope / crossing / environment:** Tobler's function (6·exp(−3.5|S+0.05|)) is the standard slope adjustment and is implemented in QGIS slope-adjusted accessibility tools alongside Naismith–Langmuir; lab evidence (Xie et al. 2024) finds ≤6% grades have negligible speed effect and >6° a significant one; TfNSW implicitly reserves >1.5 for non-"normal effort" walking; the Dutch 2022 VoT study provides formal multiplicative VTT multipliers for walking-path configuration and pavement type (values not retrieved); number of street crossings reduces perceived safety and green environments reportedly double acceptable walking time (single-study, low confidence).
