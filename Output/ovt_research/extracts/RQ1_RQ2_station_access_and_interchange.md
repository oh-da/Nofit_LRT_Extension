# Evidence review: station access / boarding penalties (RQ1) and pure interchange penalties (RQ2) — Haifa LRT parameter review

## 0. How this was done and what it is worth

50 distinct WebSearch queries were run (English, Dutch and Hebrew), after which the session's search budget was exhausted. WebFetch was blocked by the egress policy for every host tried (eprints.whiterose.ac.uk, brt.cl, nationaltransport.ie, in addition to the hosts the caller listed), so **no document was read in full**. Every number below appears verbatim in a search-engine summary or snippet; the verification status of every row is therefore "search-summary (unverified)". Three known hazards of this method, which I flag in the rows concerned: (a) search summaries sometimes attach one paper's number to a neighbouring paper (I caught two such cases and did not record them); (b) attribution of a few numbers is uncertain (marked "attribution uncertain"); (c) I did not compute any derived numbers (e.g. "60% more than 4.7 min") — only values stated in a snippet are tabulated. Values that must be confirmed by reading the source before entering a model are marked with "CONFIRM".

Streams: S1 = official guidance; S2 = meta/review; S3 = primary empirical study; S4 = operational model parameter.
Quality (Q) 1–3 and Haifa applicability (A) 1–3 are my judgement (3 = best).

## 2. Synthesis

### RQ2 — pure (net of walk & wait) interchange penalties by type
All medians below are my arithmetic on the unverified snippet values; they are indicative only.

- **Same-platform / cross-platform (rail↔rail or metro↔metro):** Yap et al. 2024 London 3.6 min (R11); RailCorp Sydney 5 min "no platform change" (R25); ATAP within-mode same-facility 5 min (R33); Douglas & Thornton appraisal 5 min (R30); Douglas & Jones 2013 cross-platform 9 min (R26). Median ≈ 5 min, range 3.6–9. Consistent relative finding: cross-platform is 20–25% (London RP) or about 2 min (Sydney SP) less than a level-change interchange.
- **Level-change rail/metro interchange:** Yap 2024 4.7 (R12); Guo & Wilson 4.9 (R16, attribution to confirm); Douglas 6.7 and RailCorp 7 (R25); ATAP within-mode different-facility 7 (R33); Wardman et al. rail–rail 8 (R02); Currie review ≈8 (R24); Douglas & Jones 11 (R27). Median ≈ 7 min, range 4.7–11.
- **Bus↔bus (street / bus station):** Wardman et al. 4.5, 3.6 if guaranteed (R01/R04); ATAP same-mode 6, or 5/7 (R31/R33); Dublin SP 16–21 (R36); Douglas & Jones 21 (R29); Currie review ≈22 (R23); Yap 2024 "≈60% more negative than metro–metro" (R13). The distribution is bimodal: UK/ATAP guidance-oriented values 4–7 min versus recent SP studies 16–22 min. Median of the five point values ≈ 18 min; range 3.6–22.
- **Bus↔rail/metro/LRT (mode change, usually with a level change):** ATAP between-mode 7 (same facility) / 10 (different facility) (R34); Yap bus–metro 10.3 (R14, attribution to confirm); Garcia-Martinez first-transfer 15.2 with +3.3 for bus↔metro (R07); Douglas & Jones 17 (R28); international review "different mode" 16, IQR 11–22 (R35). Median ≈ 12–15 min, range 7–17.
- **BRT↔LRT specifically:** no direct estimate exists in anything retrieved; Currie 2005 states explicitly that no BRT transfer-penalty values could be found. Closest proxies: ATAP between-mode same-facility 7 min; NL tram↔bus 4.8 min (R47); London cross-platform metro 3.6 min; Sydney cross-platform 5–9 min. A defensible Haifa parameter set would be about 4–5 min for a same-platform BRT↔LRT interchange and 7–10 min for one with a level change, with sensitivity tests up to the 15–17 min SP values.
- **Guidance values (pure):** TAG M3.2 5–10 min per interchange and 2–10 min boarding/transfer (R37/R38); STOPS 5 min per boarding, up to 10 (R40); ATAP 5–10 (R31–R34); VTPI 5–15 (R46); literature span 4–20 (R15).

### RQ2 — TOTAL penalties (including walk/wait or transfer time)
Schakenbos 2016: optimum 8-min BTM→train transfer carries ≈29 min generalised time; BTM→train ≈10 min worse than train→train (R08). NS 2015: 23 min reference incl. 2 min transfer time, <14 min under optimal (cross-platform, high-frequency) conditions; NS/ProRail previously used a flat 10 min (R09). Wardman 2001 meta: train→train ≈18 min apart from wait (R06). Nielsen 2021 Copenhagen 5.4–12.1 bus-IVT min depending on transfer attributes incl. escalators (R18). Seoul smartcard 11.24 min avg, 1.16–13.55 across districts (R21). Guo & Wilson 2004 Boston 2.3–21.4 walking-min equivalent (R17). Wellington model composite boarding+transfer+fare proxy 20.5–23 min (R44/R45). MVA 1991 inter-urban 32 min (R05). To convert TOTAL to PURE, Wardman weights of 1.66 (walk) and 1.47 (wait) × IVT (R53) or the Sydney transfer-wait weight 1.08 (R25) can be applied.

### RQ1 — vertical access, station and boarding penalties
- **Per-level vertical access in models:** FTA STOPS adds 0.5 min per level (0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 min for 1 / 2 / 3 levels) to grade-separated stations, plus an optional station-specific WALK_PEN and, from v1.50, explicit vertical-profile within-station walk times (R41). Sound Transit codes rail stations with explicit pedestrian and escalator links, a 0.5 min escalator-link time penalty, and factors all out-of-vehicle time by 2.0 (R43). These are the only explicit "minutes per level / per escalator" model conventions found.
- **Boarding penalties by mode:** Sound Transit: bus stop 5.0 min vs rail station 2.0 min, with wait-time factors 0.60 vs 0.50 (R42/R43); STOPS 5 min per boarding regardless of mode (R40); TAG 2–10 min boarding/transfer (R38); Wellington: bus boarding 10 (5 at high-quality interchange) vs rail 3 min (R44/R45). The common pattern is a rail/LRT boarding penalty well below the bus one (roughly 2–3 vs 5 min) that absorbs the "rail bonus"; the NL tram bonus (1 bus min ≈ 0.6 tram min, R47) is the same effect expressed as an IVT weight.
- **Behavioural value of a level change:** London RP: level-change metro interchange 4.7 vs cross-platform 3.6 min, i.e. the level change is worth about 1 min or 20–25% of the penalty (R11/R12); Sydney SP: platform change adds 2 min (9→11) (R26/R27); Copenhagen: transfer attributes incl. escalator presence move the penalty across a 5.4–12.1 min range (R18); Shanghai: interchange time perceived at 1.122× actual (R20). Guo & Wilson 2011 model walking speed separately for level walking, escalator, stairs and lift and find escalator availability among the significant penalty drivers (R16), but the component coefficients were not visible in any snippet and must be read from the PDF.
- **Physical station access times:** typical escalator rides are ≈1.5 min for a mid-depth station and up to 2.5–3 min for very deep ones (R50); fare-gate processing is ≈0.7–2 s per passenger, i.e. negligible per person unless queuing (R51); LRT dwell 14–36 s per stop and 1.4–8 s per boarding passenger (R52). For a Haifa underground LRT station, an access time of roughly 1–3 min platform↔street per direction (walk + one or two escalators + gate) is consistent with these physical figures, before any behavioural weighting (×2.0 in Sound Transit; walk 1.66× in Wardman).
- **Guidance gap:** neither TAG M3.2 nor ATAP M1 was found to give an explicit "station penalty" for underground vs surface stations; the UK/Australian practice is to code the vertical access as weighted walk time plus the boarding/interchange penalty. PDFH treats rail interchange penalties as distance- and ticket-type-dependent GJT elements, not fixed minutes (R39).
