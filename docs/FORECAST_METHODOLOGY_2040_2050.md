# Growing the 2022 TAZ matrices to 2040 and 2050 — BU and HS scenarios

*Methodology for step 23 (`notebooks/current/Forecast_matrices_TAZ_2040_2050.ipynb`), 21 September 2026.
Replaces the 25-area composite forecast of `Forecast_matrices_2040_2050.ipynb` (historical).*

## 1. What is being produced

Four **demographic reference** matrix sets at the 778-TAZ level, AM 06:00–09:00:

| Scenario-year | Zonal file | Products |
|---|---|---|
| BU 2040 | `Input/Demographic_Forecast/Zonal_BU_2040.csv` | car, transit (bus + rail), taxi-type, total (car + transit) |
| BU 2050 | `Zonal_BU_2050.csv` | same |
| HS 2040 | `Zonal_HS_2040.csv` | same |
| HS 2050 | `Zonal_HS_2050.csv` | same |

"Demographic reference" means: the 2022 base (`Output/final_2022/`) is moved to the
scenario-year population and employment with **trip rates, destination-choice
behaviour and mode split held at their 2022 values**. The matrices show where the
2022 travel pattern goes when the people and jobs move; they contain no service change,
no LRT, no change in car ownership, fares, telework or age structure. A build / no-build
comparison needs the service-response model of `docs/LRT_CAPTURE_PLAN.md` on top of
these; they are its "do-nothing" reference, not a forecast of LRT demand.

## 2. Inputs

| Input | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `Output/final_2022/{car,transit}_2022_taz.csv`, `Output/ths2017/three_mode_2022/taxi_2022_taz.csv` | the 2022 base layers (step 22 / 16) | seed and 2022 margins, per layer |
| `Input/Zonal_2020.csv`, `Input/Zonal_BU_2025.csv` | observed 2020 and BU-2025 population / employment per TAZ | the 2022 demographic level (bridge) |
| `Input/Demographic_Forecast/Zonal_{BU,HS}_{2040,2050}.csv` | scenario population (`POPULATION`) and employment (`EMPL_TOT`) per TAZ | growth targets |
| `Input/taz_keys_from_shapefile.csv` (or the LFS keys table) | TAZ → superzone | superzone patterns and rates |
| `Input/Submatrix_tazs.xlsx`, `Output/ths2017/study_taz/submatrices/area_legend.csv` | corridor areas and line sequence | reporting and corridor profiles |

The 2022 demographic level is not observed. It is bridged as
`X_2022 = X_2020 · (X_2025 / X_2020)^(2/5)` (geometric interpolation two years into the
2020–2025 BU trend), the same convention that moved the survey from 2018 to 2022
(METHODOLOGY §6n). Both scenarios use this bridge; they diverge from 2022 onwards.

## 3. Definitions

For each layer m ∈ {car, transit, taxi-type}, TAZ i (origin) and j (destination):

- `T_ij` — the 2022 matrix of layer m; `O_i = Σ_j T_ij`, `D_j = Σ_i T_ij`.
- `P_i`, `E_i` — population and employment of TAZ i in 2022; `P_i^y`, `E_i^y` in scenario-year y.
- `ΔP_i = P_i^y − P_i`, `ΔE_i = E_i^y − E_i`.
- Land-use indices `I_i = c_P P_i + c_E E_i` (production), `J_j = a_P P_j + a_E E_j`
  (attraction), fitted on total 2022 demand (4.1); `ΔI_i`, `ΔJ_j` their scenario-year changes.
- Superzone rates per layer: `r_A = Σ_{i∈A} O_i / Σ_{i∈A} I_i`, `s_B = Σ_{j∈B} D_j / Σ_{j∈B} J_j`.
- Superzone pattern per layer: `Π_AB = T_AB / T_A·` (share of A's trips going to B) and
  its reverse `Π'_BA = T_AB / T_·B`.

## 4. Steps

### 4.1 Margins — where the growth goes

AM trip ends are not generated by residents alone (origins) and jobs alone
(destinations): schools, shops, homes receiving return trips and non-home-based travel
all attract morning trips, and a TAZ whose arrivals are driven by a school does not triple
its arrivals when a small employment base triples. Without a purpose split (open task A3)
the study uses two **composite land-use indices**, fitted once on the total 2022 demand
(car + transit + taxi-type) by non-negative least squares over the 778 TAZs:

```
production index   I_i = c_P · P_i + c_E · E_i
attraction index   J_j = a_P · P_j + a_E · E_j
```

On the 2022 base the fit gives 0.51 origins per resident and 0.12 per job, and 0.26
destinations per resident and 0.81 per job (one job ≈ 3 residents on the attraction
side; R² ≈ 0.5 at TAZ level, printed by the notebook and saved in
`landuse_indices.csv`). The same indices are used for every layer: they describe the
land use's trip-end generating power, not a mode. (Fitted per layer, the transit
attractions come out almost entirely job-driven — plausible, but on a thin and diffuse
layer — so one common index is kept and the difference between modes is carried by
each layer's own pattern, not by its margins.)

Origin targets, per layer:

```
O_i^y = O_i + ρ_i · ΔI_i           with   ρ_i = O_i / I_i     if TAZ i is "established"
                                           ρ_i = r_A(i)       otherwise,  r_A = Σ_{i∈A} O_i / Σ_{i∈A} I_i
```

An origin TAZ is *established* when its 2022 index is worth at least 500 residents and
its index growth factor is at most 3. For an established TAZ the rule is the ordinary
multiplicative one (`O_i^y = O_i · I_i^y / I_i`). For a small-base or transforming TAZ —
the HS residential conversions (Matam 390 → 9,700 residents, Neot Peres, Kiryat Nahum),
new neighbourhoods, anything below the threshold in 2022 — the new residents and jobs
generate trips at their **superzone's** rate per index unit rather than at the noise of
the TAZ's own few trips. Targets are floored at zero.

Destination targets follow the same rule with the attraction index: `D_j^y = D_j + σ_j ·
ΔJ_j`, σ_j the TAZ's own trips per index unit when established, else the superzone's.
Destination targets are then rescaled so that both margins sum to the origin-side total,
as in the 2022 vintage step (productions govern the total).

**Why composite indices and not employment alone for attractions.** On a dry run
(2022 → BU-2025, a 4.7 % growth in car trips) employment-only attractions moved the car
matrix's mean centroid trip length by +8 %, because TAZs with school- or home-type
arrivals and a small, fast-growing job count received targets two to three times their
2022 arrivals; with the composite index and the seed of 4.2 the drift is +4 % (7.32 →
7.61 km), the transit layer's length is unchanged (9.55 → 9.49 km). **Why not the 25-area margins of the earlier
notebook.** The review (§13) showed that equal area totals hide where homes and jobs sit
relative to stations; TAZ margins keep that. **Why not the 2022 step's superzone fallback
for every small TAZ.** That fallback would suppress exactly the growth the HS scenario is
about; the rate rule keeps the growth and only borrows the *rate*.

### 4.2 Seed — how new demand is distributed

Furness on the 2022 matrix alone cannot put demand into cells that are empty in 2022,
and a TAZ that had 390 residents has a nearly empty row. The seed therefore has three
parts:

```
S_ij = T_ij                                            (existing demand keeps its 2022 pattern)
     + ΔO_i⁺ · T_ij / O_i                              (established origin: growth along its own 2022 row)
     + ΔO_i⁺ · Π_{A(i)B(j)} · D_j^y / D_{B(j)}^y      (small-base / transforming origin: growth follows the layer's
                                                        superzone pattern, spread within the destination superzone by targets)
     + ΔD_j⁺ · T_ij / D_j                              (established destination: growth along its own 2022 column)
     + ΔD_j⁺ · Π'_{B(j)A(i)} · O_i^y / O_{A(i)}^y     (new / small-base destination: reverse superzone pattern,
                                                        spread within the origin superzone by origin targets)
```

where `ΔO_i⁺ = max(0, O_i^y − O_i)`, `ΔD_j⁺ = max(0, D_j^y − D_j)`, and each origin or
destination takes exactly one of its two terms according to the established / small-base
classification of 4.1. The synthetic terms are the layer's own superzone-level
destination choice (car and transit have different ones — the car-vs-transit PCA of step
21 is the reason the layers are grown separately), applied only where a TAZ's own 2022
row or column cannot carry the growth. Rows or columns whose seed is empty but whose
target is positive receive the synthetic pattern outright. Confining the synthetic
pattern to the small-base zones matters: applied to every increment it pulls growth
towards the superzone-average pattern and away from each TAZ's intra-zonal
concentration, which lengthened trips by a further 3 % in the dry run.

### 4.3 Balancing

Iterative proportional fitting (Furness) of `S` to the margins of 4.1, per layer and
scenario-year, to a relative tolerance of 10⁻⁶ on every margin, at most 500 iterations.
The result `T_ij^y` is the layer's scenario-year matrix. Total = car + transit; the
taxi-type layer is kept apart as in the 2022 deliverables.

### 4.4 Mode split

Nothing is done to it. Each layer grows with the same demographic margins, so the mode
split of each origin TAZ stays at its 2022 value by construction, and the transit
matrix keeps its own (more Haifa-bound, less local) destination pattern rather than
inheriting the car pattern. This is the correct content of a demographic reference; a
mode-share change is a behavioural assumption that belongs in the service-response
step, not here.

## 5. Checks that are run and reported

1. **Margins and sign.** Every origin and destination target met within tolerance;
   no negative cells; layer sums add to the total.
2. **Superzone consistency.** The TAZ result reaggregated to superzone OD blocks is
   compared with a plain superzone-level Furness of the 2022 superzone matrix to the
   same superzone margins; differences show where the seed's synthetic growth terms
   moved demand across superzones.
3. **Trip-length distribution.** Mean and median centroid distance per layer, 2022
   versus scenario-year; a drift beyond a few percent is flagged, since the demographic
   reference should not change trip lengths much unless growth is placed far from jobs.
4. **Implied trip rates.** Trips per resident by superzone, 2022 versus scenario-year —
   near-constant where growth is ordinary; the table shows what the small-base rule and
   the job component of the indices did.
5. **Corridor classes and profiles.** Totals by corridor class, and the three-hour
   potential-movement profile along the 18 line areas for total and transit, per
   scenario-year, next to 2022.
6. **BU versus HS.** Per-TAZ and per-area differences, with the Tirat Carmel / Matam /
   Neot Peres / Kiryat Nahum cases called out.

## 6. What the results can and cannot support

They support: comparing scenario-years and scenarios on the same behavioural basis;
locating where growth adds demand along the corridor; the demographic component of an
LRT market. They do not support: LRT ridership, mode shift, peak loads (apply the step-20
factors for a departure-hour view, but the caveats of §6r remain), or any statement that
depends on travel behaviour changing between 2022 and the scenario-year. Every 2022
caveat carries forward — the bus layer's coverage threshold, the purpose-blind TAZ
allocation, the missing external trips, the absence of external validation — and two
are added: the constant trip-rate assumption (ageing, car ownership and telework are not
represented), and the superzone-rate rule for small-base TAZs, whose effect is reported
and can be varied (threshold 500 residents, factor 3).

## 7. Sensitivities to run alongside

- Small-base threshold (250 / 500 / 1,000 residents' worth of index) and growth-factor cut-off (2 / 3 / 5).
- Attraction index: employment-only versus the fitted composite (the dry run shows the trip-length consequence).
- Seed growth terms off (pure Furness on the 2022 matrix) — shows what the synthetic
  terms add; with them off, new-development TAZs receive no trips.
- Destination-side total governed by employment instead of population.
- Peak-hour view: the step-20 factors applied to the scenario-year link profiles.
